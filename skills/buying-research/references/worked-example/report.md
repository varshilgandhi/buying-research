# Worked example: buy the Rizta S, lock its battery cover

> **This shows the method. It is not buying advice.** It is condensed and anonymised from one real purchase, researched in October 2026 in one Indian city. Prices, warranties and evidence will have changed. The buyer, city and local businesses are removed. The original read a real personal loan offer, so the loan figures here are replaced by illustrations at one listed rate.
>
> **Buyer:** one rider doing about 50 km a day, 16,000 km a year, in city traffic, keeping the scooter 8+ years, budget about Rs 1.3-1.55 lakh, likely on a loan.

**Pick: Ather Rizta S 123 km with AtherStack Pro and the 8-year / 80,000 km battery plan, Rs 1,49,042 on-road. Confidence: Medium.**
**Fallback: Bajaj Chetak C3503 with TECpac, stand and footrest, Rs 1,46,031.**

The win is narrow, and it is not about range, because all of these clear 50 km a day. For Rs 3,011 more, the Rizta gives a front disc against drum brakes, 80 km/h against 63-70, and battery cover to 5 years against about 4.4. The accounts we read show no confirmed pack failure on it, while the 35-series Chetak has many early ones, usually replaced free. Its weaknesses are real: belts that can snap, poor service reports at several centres, the smallest range margin, and a 70% health promise with no known payout. Nobody has run either scooter for 8 years, so this is a judgement, not a proof. Do not book until the test rides and written answers below are done. No dated festival offer was found, so there is no reason to rush.

**How to read this.** [Official] is the maker's page, [Owner] is an unverified owner story, [Ours] is our calculation or judgement. Complaint counts are not failure rates. Unhappy owners post more, and TVS alone built about 10 lakh iQubes ([Rushlane](https://www.rushlane.com/tvs-iqube-crosses-10-lakh-production-milestone-in-india-12549811.html?nonamp=1)). ## What would flip the pick

To the C3503 [Ours]: the nearby service bays disappoint; Ather will not put the pack and plan in writing; the rider dislikes the ride; or Ather's all-in loan total is more than about Rs 12,000-15,000 above the Chetak's.

**Two open questions**: is the 10-year plan really on sale, and which pack is in this variant? Ather's page prices the plan but says sale "will begin soon", and the price page does not name the pack. A dealer who will not write down the pack does not get the money.

## What it costs over 8 years

Over 8 years the Rizta costs roughly Rs 20,000 more than the C3503 once belts and service are counted [Ours, rounded], so cost does not decide this. Insurance renewals and tyres are left out because no reliable figure exists. A new battery is extra on any of them, and a labelled guess of Rs 65,000-75,000.

## Where to buy and get service

Many five-star counts look like delivery-day or solicited reviews, which is our inference. So service-centre ratings were weighed over showroom ones, and the text under the number was read. In one chain, a centre rated 4.2 for sales scored 2.8 for service, and owners there described 3-day jobs and unfixed faults. That split is the warning. Names and details are in the buyer's private copy.

## Before paying

No dated offer was found, so there is no rush. Test ride the pick and fallback, with firm braking from 50-60 km/h. Visit the service bays, and ask how many scooters are waiting for parts today. Get itemised written quotes and the lender's Key Fact Statement. Pay a booking only with refund terms in writing.

**Ask in writing:**
1. Which pack, kWh and chemistry, is in this variant, and will the invoice say so?
2. Is the 10-year plan on sale today? Is its price instead of the 8-year plan, or on top?
3. How is 70% health measured? Has any centre here replaced a battery under it?
4. Are belts covered, up to what km, and how many are in stock here?
5. Which lender, what is the true rate, and what are the fee and foreclosure charge?
