# Evidence rules

These rules exist because the first research this skill is based on got things wrong: wrong dealer counts from aggregators, wrong speeds, and a strong contender dropped without a reason. Official pages corrected them.

## Keep kinds of fact apart

Label them in notes and reports, so a reader can see how much to trust each claim. Use only the labels the report needs.

| Label | Means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `[Official]` | The maker's or lender's own page | Warranty is 3 years / 30,000 km |
| `[Retail]` | A seller's listing or a price tracker | Rs 19,290 at a named retailer on this date |
| `[Test]` | An independent lab or consumer body | Minimum fill measured at 245 ml |
| `[Offer]` | A personal or quoted offer for this buyer | The EMI on their own offer screen |
| `[Owner]` | An unverified owner account | Belt snapped at 33,500 km |
| `[Ours]` | Our calculation or judgement | Cover ends at 5.0 years for this usage |

## Say how each source was read

Mark weak reads, since they carry less weight:

- `[read]` you saw the page text itself, for example in Chrome
- `[summary]` a fetch tool returned a model's summary of the page
- `[auto-captions]` video not watched, numbers may be garbled
- `[snippet]` search summary only, page not opened

Search and fetch summaries can state opinions as fact, and they can be stale. Never present a snippet or summary as if the page was read, and re-read the page before relying on one for a deciding fact.

## Reading the evidence

- **Complaint counts are not failure rates.** Unhappy owners post more. Give the sold or built volume where you can find it, and never turn counts into a percentage.
- **Absence of failures is not proof.** A clean record on a two-year-old product says little about year eight.
- **Reports may not transfer.** Stories from an older design say little about the new one, in either direction.
- **Surveys have limits.** Check who was asked and over what period. A dealer survey is not a customer survey, and a first-six-months quality study is not a long-term one.
- **Ratings may be pooled.** Marketplaces can merge years and variants under one listing, so check that reviews are for this exact model.
- **Reviews may be seeded.** Warning signs on any site include polished or short five-star reviews bunched in a few weeks, delivery-day praise, and praise for one staff member. Say this is your inference.
- **Tests of a related model are not tests of this one.** Label them `[Test, sibling]` and say what differs.
- **Seller-built specs need checking.** If a listing beats every maker build, find out who fitted the part and whether the maker's warranty survives.
- **Promotional content is not evidence of quality.** Tag paid, dealer and creator content, and note affiliate links. Sellers' blogs and scripted channels with no hands-on use are also promotion.
- **Conflicting sources stay visible.** State both and which one you believe, or ask the buyer to confirm with the seller.
- **Estimates are labelled.** A guess at a future repair cost is `[Ours]`, with its basis stated.
- **Quote specs as printed.** If a listing looks mislabelled, such as a height shown as a width, give the printed values and your reading of them as `[Ours]`. Do not build a conclusion on the corrected version without saying so.

## Comparing fairly

- Compare the same thing: what the buyer must really buy, on the same basis, on the same date.
- Do not let a difference that clears the buyer's need decide anything.
- Check named options properly, even if they seem weak. Someone in the family may ask about them.

## Honesty about coverage

- **Say what could not be read,** by name and with the reason. Never imply coverage of a blocked platform.
- **State the pick's weaknesses** as plainly as its strengths. No product is perfect.
- **Do not rush them.** A festival or sale matters only if a dated offer was found.
- **Correct yourself openly.** If a later finding overturns something the user was told, say so and keep it in the corrections list.
